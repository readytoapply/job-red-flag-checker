## How these cases were written

- **Blind to the rules.** The cases were written without opening, reading or searching the checker's rules (`src/`) or its tests (`test/`). No case was written to hit or dodge a particular pattern. Each one is meant to read the way real scams and real job postings read.
- **Composed, not copied.** Every text was written from scratch, based on the scam patterns these public pages describe. No sentence is taken from a source. The `source` field on a case names the page that describes the pattern the case shows. On a legitimate case, it names the page for the practice shown (for example, a state fingerprinting fee or Form I-9 documents). 90 of the 110 legitimate cases have no source (`null`) because they show ordinary hiring practice. Four scam cases have no source because the page they cited is no longer available.
- **Invented people and companies.** All names of people, companies, domains and handles are invented. No real company or brand appears as an employer or as an impersonation target. Phone numbers use the reserved 555-01xx range.
- **Who wrote them.** The cases were drafted by an AI assistant (Claude).
- **How the sources were checked.** The pages were found by web search on the access date. Three pages were read in full: the IC3 PSAs of 2024 and 2020 and the USPIS reshipping page. For the rest, the case relies on the page's title and the summary the search returned.
- **Splits.** Within each `type`, cases alternate between `dev` and `test` in the order they were written. That makes each type about half and half.
